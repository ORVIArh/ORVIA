# Angular functions, harmonic angles and periodic torsions

The v0.3 angular lane supplies three deployable numerical components. They use the existing `FloatMath` contract and its binary128-format arithmetic. Their limits and test tolerances are engineering specifications; they do not establish a production simulation error budget or full correctly-rounded IEEE 754 behavior.

## ElementaryMath

Deploy `ElementaryMath(mathAddress)`. The immutable `numericalMath` dependency is exposed publicly, along with binary128 constants `PI`, `TWO_PI`, `HALF_PI` and `MAX_TRIG_ARGUMENT` (32*pi). The constants are nearest binary128 representations of those values.

| Method | Defined domain and result |
|---|---|
| `atan2(y,x)` | Finite inputs except (0,0); principal angle in [-pi,pi] |
| `sinCos(angle)` | Finite absolute argument at most 32*pi; returns sine then cosine |
| `acos(x)` | Finite x in [-1,1]; result in [0,pi] |

Signed zeros are numerical zeros. Thus the negative-x-axis result for y equal to either signed zero is +pi. Callers requiring IEEE signed-zero branch conventions must not assume them here.

`atan2` uses a magnitude ratio no larger than one, a half-angle reduction, and 48 terms of an alternating arctangent series. The reduced argument is at most sqrt(2)-1. The omitted series term is below 1e-38 in exact arithmetic; actual computation additionally includes binary128 rounding and the pinned upstream arithmetic behavior.

Sine and cosine first reduce a bounded argument into [-pi,pi] in at most 16 full-turn subtractions/additions, then evaluate 33 Taylor terms including the initial term. Exact-arithmetic truncation is much smaller than binary128 precision on this interval. Range reduction uses the stored rounded pi constants, so outputs near multiples of pi are not promised to be mathematical zeros. No unbounded-argument range-reduction claim is made.

## HarmonicAngleKernel

Deploy `HarmonicAngleKernel(mathAddress, elementaryAddress)`. The constructor checks that the elementary contract references the same arithmetic contract.

```solidity
evaluate(a, b, c, k, theta0)
    -> (energy, forceA, forceB, forceC)
```

Positions and returned vectors are `bytes16[3]`; scalar numerical parameters are `bytes16`. Atom b is the center. The energy is `k/2 * (theta-theta0)^2`, with `theta = atan2(|(a-b) cross (c-b)|, (a-b) dot (c-b))`. The analytic Cartesian forces are negative energy gradients. Parameters require finite k >= 0 and theta0 in [0,pi]. Zero-length arms and collinear geometries reject, including when k is zero.

The force calculation uses dot/cross products directly to avoid forming an inverse cosine of a rounded normalized dot product. This does not remove the physical/angular singularity at collinearity or guarantee conditioning near it. A molecular protocol must keep its accepted geometric domain away from unresolved numerical singularities.

## PeriodicTorsionKernel

Deploy `PeriodicTorsionKernel(mathAddress, elementaryAddress)` with the same dependency check.

```solidity
evaluate(a, b, c, d, periodicity, k, phase)
    -> (energy, forceA, forceB, forceC, forceD)
```

Periodicity is `uint32` in 1..6; k is finite and nonnegative; phase is finite in [-2*pi,2*pi]. Both planes must be nondegenerate. The middle bond and both adjacent cross-product normals must have nonzero length. Zero-strength terms still validate geometry.

The torsion convention is fixed as follows:

```text
b1 = b-a; b2 = c-b; b3 = d-c
n1 = b1 cross b2; n2 = b2 cross b3
phi = atan2((b2/|b2|) dot (n1 cross n2), n1 dot n2)
U = k * (1 + cos(periodicity*phi - phase))
```

This convention has phi=0 for a planar cis arrangement and magnitude pi for planar trans. The implementation differentiates the dot/cross/atan2 expression analytically using the reverse chain rule. A force-field import must validate its phase and orientation convention explicitly; the shared scalar energy form is not a claim that all external topology conventions are interchangeable.

Both force kernels receive unwrapped nonperiodic coordinates. They perform no periodic image selection, parameter assignment, exclusions, topology accumulation, integration or complete force-field selection themselves.

## Independent evidence

`reference/generate_angular_vectors.py` uses mpmath 1.3.0 at 100 decimal digits. Its force references are generated by differentiating independent scalar energy expressions with `mpmath.diff`, not by translating the Solidity force algebra. It produces ten atan2 cases, ten sine/cosine cases, four angle cases and six torsion cases, including general geometry, signed quadrants, cis/trans planes, high periodicity and zero strengths. Binary128 inputs are interpreted at their actual encoded values.

Regenerate with the pinned research interpreter:

```bash
.venv-research/bin/python reference/generate_angular_vectors.py
```

`test/angular.test.mjs` compares contract output directly as decoded binary128 against those references, using absolute 1e-29 plus relative 1e-27 tolerance. It also checks contract-energy finite differences, translation/reversal, total force and torque, parameter domains and singular geometry. The completed outcomes and measured errors are in `reports/angular_validation.json` and the combined EVM test log.

The standard scalar energy forms are described in the [OpenMM theory guide](https://docs.openmm.org/latest/userguide/theory/02_standard_forces.html), sections HarmonicAngleForce and PeriodicTorsionForce. ORVIA's algorithm, orientation convention, numerical limits and validation evidence are specified above rather than inferred from that reference.
